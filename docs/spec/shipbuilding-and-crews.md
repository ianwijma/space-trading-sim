# Shipbuilding and crews

Status: warships consume local workers by taking them aboard as crew, allowing
asteroids to specialize in producing ships. This is a confirmed requirement.
Crew sizes, commissioning details, losses, and demobilization below are proposals.

## Two uses of workers

Construction-harbour workers build the hull. They remain in the asteroid's shared
workforce and operate under the same coverage ratio as every other staffed
building. A completed warship needs a separate complement of people to sail it.

At commissioning, those people leave the local civilian population and become
embarked crew. They no longer operate asteroid buildings, consume local household
supplies, or occupy asteroid housing. Their living space is aboard the ship.
Do not add crew size to building demand R or deduct the shipyard's builders again.

Crew is a population commitment paid once at commissioning. Movement, repairs,
combat orders, and ordinary docking do not consume another complement of workers.
Onboard accommodation and life support are abstracted into the vessel design in
the first release; supplying military food and water at sea is an expansion.

## Proposed crew complements

| Warship | People required aboard | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Interceptor | 5 | A small but meaningful early workforce commitment |
| Frigate | 10 | A larger commitment for escort and fleet combat |
| Cruiser | 20 | A major commitment suited to established shipbuilding colonies |

These are tunable values, separate from construction-harbour staffing, material
costs, build work, and combat strength. No training building or crew profession is
required initially: any housed local worker can become crew.

Civilian freighters and market ships are automated in the proposed first release.
Passenger and colony vessels carry explicit people as cargo manifests; those
people are moved from their source and never generated by manufacturing a hull.
Captured civilian ships consequently need no additional military crew transfer.
Crews for civilian ships and prize crews are possible expansions.

## Construction and commissioning

1. Queue a hull with a material cost, construction-work requirement, and full crew
   requirement. The preview shows the workforce impact if it launched now.
2. Build it using local materials and the harbour's shared staffing rate. Queueing
   or building reserves no people and does not create extra worker demand.
3. When construction finishes, mark the project ready for commissioning. A new
   hull cannot commission until the next economic boundary.
4. At that boundary, check that the shipyard is usable, the launch is enabled,
   and enough housed local residents exist for the entire crew.
5. In one identified transaction, subtract the crew from local population, create
   its ship crew manifest, and commission the military vessel. Release the bay.
6. Rehouse any displaced residents using the resulting free places, then calculate
   the asteroid's shared workforce ratio for the coming economic interval.

A commissioned ship is fully crewed. Partial staffing is supported for buildings;
an incomplete ship crew keeps the hull waiting. This makes the population cost
visible and avoids a separate per-ship staffing slider.

If people are missing, show "Hull complete — waiting for 3 crew." The completed
project occupies its construction bay until commissioning or cancellation. An
enabled construction harbour still contributes its normal building-worker demand
while waiting. More construction harbours provide more independent bays, subject
to the same local population and supply constraints.

Manual passenger departures and demobilizations follow accepted command order;
automatic commissioning follows, ordered by hull-ready interval and stable project
ID. Recheck remaining housed residents for each launch. Two bays cannot embark
the same people. A disabled harbour retains its project and resumes when usable.

Players may hold a launch or cancel a queued project. Cancellation returns only
unconsumed reserved materials; completed work is spent. Before commissioning
there is no crew to refund. After commissioning, use demobilization rather than
cancelling a construction order to get people back.

## Workforce effect

```text
Before launch: P = 20, H >= 20, building demand R = 20 -> C = 100%
Embark 5:      P = 15, same H and R                  -> C = 75%
```

Every staffed building, including the shipyard, now runs at 75% of its staffing
capacity before other limits. Growth or arriving passengers can restore it over
time. A launch may leave coverage unchanged if there were spare workers or
displaced people ready to occupy the freed beds; use the actual W/R calculation.

There is no automatic requirement to preserve full industrial staffing. The player
can accept a temporary slowdown, hold the launch, build housing in advance, or
bring people from another asteroid. A fully emptied settlement needs functional
automatic housing, power, and reserves to recover through gradual growth.

## A ship-producing asteroid

A large asteroid can devote slots to habitats, construction harbours, power,
storage, and freight handling. Supply colonies deliver alloys, electronics,
propellant, food, and water. Local growth and passenger arrivals replace the
workers who leave aboard completed ships.

Sustained output is constrained by construction work, supplied materials, crew
availability, and growth. Adding shipyards alone can increase worker demand and
make replenishment the bottleneck. Roughly, in a stable population, new ships/min
cannot exceed net available people/min divided by that class's crew requirement;
keep enough people ashore to sustain the intended construction throughput.

Show the next launch's population cost and projected coverage, current growth,
waiting hulls, and the estimated crew-limited production rate. Estimates must
state assumptions about support and incoming passengers.

## Crew lifecycle

| Event | Population effect |
| --- | --- |
| Queue or construct hull | None; builders remain residents |
| Commission warship | Move its complement from local housed residents to its manifest |
| Dock, refuel, repair, patrol, or capture civilian cargo | Crew remains aboard; no local worker refund |
| Ship takes ordinary damage | Crew count unchanged in the initial abstraction |
| Warship destroyed | Proposed rule: record its crew as casualties and remove the manifest exactly once |
| Demobilize at an owned usable freight harbour | Remove the ship and return its crew once, provided enough free usable housing exists |

Demobilization requires the ship to arrive and consumes it, preventing an active
empty hull from remaining behind. Check free beds at execution time; if they are
unavailable, crew stays aboard and the order waits or is cancelled. It gives no
destruction credit or material refund. Demobilization executes at an economic
boundary; returned people join that boundary's household and workforce
calculations. A later salvage mechanic can define material recovery separately.

Military rescue pods are a possible expansion. The proposed loss rule above does
not change the existing civilian-passenger rescue rule. Military crew cannot be
captured as trade cargo, and crew losses give no additional kill or capture points.

## Accounting and scoring

Maintain one identified crew manifest per commissioned warship, with faction,
ship ID, count, source asteroid, and commissioning event. Boarding decreases local
residents and increases embarked crew equally. Only growth and explicitly recorded
losses change the faction's living total after initial setup.

Crew living aboard does not qualify as supported civilian population. A military
ship qualifies for operational fleet strength only when fully crewed and meeting
the health/fuel rules. Uncommissioned projects have no fleet strength and give no
military destruction credit. A real commissioned hull can earn destruction credit
once; its crew count does not multiply that reward.

Persist construction state, crew manifests, transfer/loss/demobilization event IDs,
and growth progress. Replaying a launch after reload or host migration cannot
remove residents twice, duplicate a ship, or refill the source population.
